	- [netherland study](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/2553566/166278_471298.pdf) of the origins of narcissism in 565 children from ages 7-11 over 1.5 years
		- parents report valuation of children and emotional warmth they show (agree/disagree statements)
		- children report emotional warmth they received (agree/disagree statements). they were measured for narcissism and self-esteem
	- children with high self-esteem do not think they are more special than others, and more often reported agreeing with statements about being happy with themselves as a person and liking the kind of person they were
	- study concludes that self-esteem and narcissism development differently; narcisissm is not overblown self-esteem (this seems to align with my understanding that narcissists are deeply insecure)
		- the children who showed a higher level of narcissism over time often correlated with parents that agreed with more child overvaluing statements. however, children with higher self-esteem were not correlated with these types of parents
		- children who with higher self-esteem over time were associated with parents who showed more emotional warmth. of children that reported receiving more emotional warmth (with either type of parent), they were not correlated to higher levels of narcissism.
	- independent of the parents' narcissism level, parental overvaluation predicted child narcissism. child narcissism is also linked with genetics and temperamental traits
		- overvaluing parents in a previous study by Brummelman, Bushman, et. al ( #needs_source ) would claim their children had knowledge on many different topics -- even nonexistent ones
		- altho not all children with overvaluing parents became narcissistic, some can become more likely than peers to do so
