	- These foods have the **[[#red]]==highest amounts of naturally occurring citric acid==**:
		- ****Lemons**
		   **Limes**
		   **Oranges**
		   **Grapefruits**
		   **Berries****
	- If you're avoiding citric acid in food, or trying to cut down on acid in food overall, stay clear of or moderate these **[[#red]]==fruits that are low on the pH scale==**:
		- Certain citrus fruits, including **lemons** (2 to 2.6 pH), **limes** (2 to 2.8 pH) and **oranges** (3.69 to 4.19 pH)
		   Some berries, such as **blueberries** (3.12 to 3.33 pH), **raspberries** (3.22 to 3.95 pH) and **strawberries** (3.0 to 3.9 pH)
		   Certain stone fruits like **peaches** (3.3 to 4.05 pH) and **plums** (2.8 to 3.4 pH)
		   **Grapes**, which range from 2.8 to 3.82 pH, depending on the variety
		   **Apples**, which range from 3.33 to 4.0 pH, depending on the variety #chemistry
	- A List of [[#red]]==Fruits With Low Citrus Acid==:
		- **Bananas**: 4.5 to 5.20 pH.
		      **Figs**: 5.05 to 5.98 pH.
		      **Watermelon**: 5.18 to 5.6 pH.
		      **Papaya**: 5.2 to 6.0 pH.
		      **Mango**: 5.8 to 6.0 pH.
		      **Honeydew Melon**: 6.0 to 6.67 pH.
		      **Cantaloupe Melon**: 6.13 to 6.58 pH.
		      **Avocados**: 6.27 to 6.58 pH.

			- [[#green]]==fat soluble== where [[$green]]==leftover== amounts are [[$green]]==stored in the liver and fat tissues as reserves==.  The fat-soluble vitamins are [[$green]]==A, D, E, and K==.
