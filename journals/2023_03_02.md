- language learning principles:
	- (1) acquisition thru comprehensible messages
		- dictionaries aid but cannot replace acquisition
		- acquisition vs learning
	- (2) pattern-recognition brain can intuit grammar from context in media
		- opt for subtitles in target language
		- read without looking up words in dictionary. use context & the words you already know
	- (3) shadowing improves listening & pronunciation
		- focus on mouth shapes and positions, pacing, intonation, cadence
		- be patient and only attempt chunks at a time -- this prevents mumbling & frustration
		- record yourself and note your good pronunciations & areas to improve
		- speaking does not necessarily support acquisition
			- speaking elicits more examples native speakers of target language
			- speaking helps with fluidity
	- (4) flashcards & memorization are unavoidable
		- master the "core 100" or most commonly used words in target language. then proceed to the next 100, and so on
		- master Tim Ferriss's "13 Sentences for introducing yourself to the Grammar"
	- language specific tips
		- JLPT: writing kanji is unnecessary. focus on reading & stroke order.
- become a speed learner
	- learns most information during doctor's visits. patients understand more about clinical condition within 3 mins. pay attention to how doctors teach patients
	- (1) safety netting: connections, emotions, implications, limits. logical backbone.
		- (1.1) foundational questions
			- what are we talking about? {essential and core sentence here}
				- what am i doing here? what am i achieving?
			- why is this important? why should we care?
			- how important is this? {dev good measure from knowing nothing to knowing everything}
			- what can i do when i learn this?
			- what can't i do even though i've learned this?
			- where can i find further knowledge?
			- who are the experts?
		- (1.2) questions to ask in lectures (instead of taking notes) to create strong foundation to connect new ideas to
			- so what we're talking about is?
			- what you actually mean is?
			- so do you mean that when this happens then this other thing happens?
	- (2) core basics: applicable, simple rules. significant & immediate.
		- what are the easiest things that i can learn most quickly?
		- what do experts do on a weekly basis?
		- what small things distinguish an amateur from a pro?
		- (2.1) identify a core skill
		- (2.2) test the core skill in many different contexts
		- (2.3) find where i can't use the core skill -- the limits
		- (2.4) strong core basics -> fast learning later
	- (3) admin & good for you
		- divide information into categories while scanning or speed reading
			- admin: hard for me to learn & less important. small things that might hold you back in process
			- good for you: don't care & unimportant. things that are irrelevant in most cases and you're not interested in. the things you are kind of interested in will fall into some of the other categories eventually. fields are endless.
	- (4) jumpy learning & strategic drowning: attention is a limited resource, maximize motivation & efficient use of energy
		- jumpy learning: study things in order of your interest, rather than the order that they are given to you. leads to longer & faster learning. jump back and forth in the way that makes sense to me. relies on core basics
		- strategic drowning: going deep.... into a topic..... to guide your studying session... look at advanced things that challenge core basics or assume firm grasp on core basics. the specifics and details are not important. focus on high-level knowledge
