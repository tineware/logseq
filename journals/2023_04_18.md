	- ### Contemporary reception
	  
	  [James Q. Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Q._Wilson "James Q. Wilson"), in a 1998 [_New York Times_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times") [Op-Ed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op-ed "Op-ed"), wrote: "If it is the work of a madman, then the writings of many political philosophers—[Jean Jacques Rousseau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Jacques_Rousseau "Jean-Jacques Rousseau"), [Tom Paine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Paine "Thomas Paine"), [Karl Marx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Marx "Karl Marx")—are scarcely more sane."[[84]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Kaczynski#cite_note-86)
	  
	  > "The Unabomber does not like socialization, technology, leftist political causes or conservative attitudes. Apart from his call for an (unspecified) revolution, his paper resembles something that a very good graduate student might have written."[[85]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Kaczynski#cite_note-87)
	  
	  Alston Chase, a fellow alumnus of [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") wrote in 2000 for _[The Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Atlantic "The Atlantic")_ that "It is true that many believed Kaczynski was insane because they needed to believe it. But the truly disturbing aspect of Kaczynski and his ideas is not that they are so foreign but that they are so familiar." He argued that "We need to see Kaczynski as exceptional—madman or genius—because the alternative is so much more frightening."[[86]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Kaczynski#cite_note-88)
	- ### Other works
	  
	  [University of Michigan–Dearborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan%E2%80%93Dearborn "University of Michigan–Dearborn") philosophy professor [David Skrbina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Skrbina "David Skrbina") helped to compile Kaczynski's work into the 2010 anthology _Technological Slavery_, including the original manifesto, letters between Skrbina and Kaczynski, and other essays.[[87]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Kaczynski#cite_note-Chronicle:_Pen_Pal-89) Kaczynski updated his 1995 manifesto as _[Anti-Tech Revolution: Why and How](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Tech_Revolution:_Why_and_How "Anti-Tech Revolution: Why and How")_ to address advances in computers and the internet. He advocates practicing other types of protest and makes no mention of violence.[[88]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Kaczynski#cite_note-90)
	  
	  According to a 2021 study, Kaczynski's manifesto "is a synthesis of ideas from three well-known academics: French philosopher [Jacques Ellul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Ellul "Jacques Ellul"), British zoologist [Desmond Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desmond_Morris "Desmond Morris"), and American psychologist [Martin Seligman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Seligman "Martin Seligman")."[[89]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Kaczynski#cite_note-:2-91)
