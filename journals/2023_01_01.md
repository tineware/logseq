	- **15:53**  [in a classroom](https://www.teachforamerica.org/stories/8-ways-to-check-for-student-understanding) article notes
		- one-question quiz: very simple quiz of one question (maaaybe two) related to the most crucial points of the lesson. can pair or group up and work together or submit answers in a box. adjust material accordingly for the next lesson
		- exit ticket: can be one-question quiz. can also be each student's summary of today's lesson or questions they still have. general feedback.
		- whiteboard explanation: encourage students to write what they have learned today on the board. together. decide how u want to guide this or just let them do it under a time limit. or have multiple whiteboards and break into groups. then go over info as a class and correct any misinfo or clarify confusions
		- pair up and talk: students take turn being the teacher. observe the weaker points of understanding with the whole class. if only one or two students need help, address them individually later.
		- interactive notebooks: integrate thinking space next to assignments; divide composition notebook into two sides. students complete assignments on right and explore material on left (like scratch paper, encouraged to jot down anything and everything that comes to mind) while doing the assignment. check periodically for understanding (decide whether u want ot grade) and adapt material accordingly
		- kahoot! or other class participation with low / no consequences: individuals or pairs or groups to get data on understanding
		- turns have tabled: quiz instructor. challenge students to create their own set of questions based on the material. encourage deeper exploration and complicated questions. show your thought process.
		- hw: assign something for later after students have had time to reflect. make sure these assignments actually check understanding. in some cases, extra time between a lesson and the assignment is enough for the material to click. adjust accordingly
