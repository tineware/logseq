		- Moravec's Paradox
			- an observation that in AI and Robotics that unlike traditional assumptions, reasoning requires very little computation while perception and sensorimotor skills require enormous amounts of resources.
			- articulated as a principle in the 1980s
			- suggested to be based on biological realities, natural selection, evolution.
				- natural selection tends to preserve design improvements and optimizations. since we have only begin to introduce abstract thinking in humans, we should expect its implementation to be particularly efficient. older skills have had more iterations of natural selection to improve.
				- abstract thinking in humans might be less than 100 thousand years old, we have not mastered it. it is not [[intrinsically]] difficult, just feels like it.
		- "it is comparatively easy to make computers exhibit adult level performance on intelligence tests or playing checkers, and difficult or impossible to give them the skills of a one-year-old when it comes to perception and mobility."
